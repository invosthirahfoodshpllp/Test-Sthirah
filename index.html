It is still sugar.
We're not going to tell you otherwise.

Jaggery is roughly 65–85% sucrose. It will raise your blood sugar. Anyone selling it to you as a health food is selling you something else as well.

What we can honestly say is this: ours is sugar that hasn't had everything stripped out of it, and nothing chemical put back in. The molasses stays — which is where the colour, the caramel flavour and the trace minerals live. That's the whole pitch.

How it's made

You can read the process off its colour.

A jaggery maker doesn't use a thermometer. He watches the pan change colour. These are the four stages our cane passes through, in the order they happen.

01

Cane crushedகரும்பு

Cut and pressed the same day. Juice sits pale and green before anything is done to it.

02

Open-pan boilகொதிக்க வைத்தல்

Boiled in a shallow iron pan over a cane-trash fire. Skimmed by hand. No sulphur, no hydros, nothing added to lighten it.

03

Thickens downபாகு

Water leaves, molasses concentrates, colour deepens to amber. This is the point most factories bleach back to pale. We don't.

04

Set and groundசர்க்கரை

Cooled, dried, ground fine and packed within 72 hours. Deep brown, because that's what unbleached jaggery looks like.

Why ours is darker than the shop's. Pale, uniform, golden jaggery usually means chemical clarification. Colour that varies slightly between batches means the cane varied — which is what happens with real cane. [ADD PHOTO/VIDEO OF YOUR PAN HERE — this section converts hardest with real footage]

Questions

The ones you were going to ask anyway.

Is it really sulphur-free? How do I know?
Every batch is tested at [NABL lab] for sulphur dioxide, and the report is published here with the batch number on it. Match the batch code printed on your pack to the report. If they don't match, tell us and we'll refund you.
Is jaggery safe for diabetics?
No — treat it as sugar. Jaggery is mostly sucrose and its glycaemic index is comparable to table sugar. If you're managing diabetes, ask your doctor. We'd rather lose the sale than have you believe otherwise.
Is it safe for children?
It's a sweetener with no chemical additives, so it's as safe as any sugar — which means the same rules apply on quantity. Not recommended for infants under one year, same as honey and sugar.
Why is yours darker than the jaggery I get locally?
Because it hasn't been bleached. Pale, glossy, uniformly golden jaggery has usually been treated with sulphur or hydros to lighten it. Unbleached jaggery is brown and varies a little between batches.
Will it dissolve properly in filter coffee?
Yes. It's ground fine and it goes into hot decoction cleanly. If you find residue at the bottom of the tumbler, something's wrong with your pack — tell us.
Why does it cost more than the ₹60/kg jaggery at my shop?
Open-pan boiling without chemical clarification takes longer and yields less. Lab testing costs money per batch. Cane from one named farm costs more than pooled cane. If price is the only thing that matters to you, buy the ₹60 one — we mean that.
It's turned into a lump. Has it gone bad?
No — jaggery absorbs moisture, and we don't add anti-caking agents. Break it up with a spoon and store it in an airtight steel dabba away from the stove.
What if I don't like it?
Tell us within 7 days and we refund the full amount. Keep the pack — sending food back and forth helps nobody.
